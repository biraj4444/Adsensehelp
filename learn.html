Understand AdSense before you add any code

What it is, what Google asks of you, which rules matter most, and a checklist that tells you when your site is ready.

5 min read8 checklist itemsBeginner friendly
Official Google informationOur recommendation

What is AdSense?

Google AdSense lets you earn from a website you own by showing ads that Google supplies. You add a small piece of code to your pages. Google chooses the ads. The program policies decide what is allowed.

That is the whole idea. Everything else on this site is about doing the technical parts correctly: putting the code in the right place, proving the site is yours, and fixing it when something does not work.

You control

Your content, your site's technical setup, and where ads are allowed on your pages.

Google controls

Whether your site is approved, which ads appear, what they pay and the policies.

Nobody controls

Traffic and earnings. Treat anyone who promises either as a warning sign.

How it works, in four moves

  1. Connect. You add your site to your AdSense account and place Google's code in your page head.
  2. Review. Google checks the site against its policies. This can take time and the result is Google's decision.
  3. Place ads. You turn on Auto ads, or create ad units and paste their code where ads should appear.
  4. Maintain. You keep the code, ads.txt and content healthy as the site changes.

What AdSense asks of you

Official Google information

  • Your content must be your own and comply with the AdSense Program policies.
  • You must be at least 18 years old. Under-18 applicants can have a parent or guardian sign up with their own Google Account.
  • You must be able to edit the HTML of the site you submit and place the AdSense code between its head tags.

Read the full pages: Eligibility requirements and Access to your site's HTML.

Our recommendation

If you build with a website builder that does not let you edit the head, check what it allows before you apply. Not being able to edit the head is a common reason people get stuck.

The rules that trip people up

The full policy is long. These are the points beginners most often break, taken from the official policy page.

Do

  • Publish original content that helps real visitors.
  • Put ads in normal content pages.
  • Test your layout on phones and desktops.
  • Re-read the policies when Google announces a change.

Don't

  • Click your own ads, or ask anyone to click or view them.
  • Encourage clicks with wording like "click the ads" or "support us".
  • Point arrows or graphics at ads.
  • Place ads in pop-ups, emails or pages with no content.

Official Google information

These come from the AdSense Program policies. Google can update them at any time, and publishers are expected to keep up to date.

Privacy policy and visitor consent

Ads use cookies and similar technology, so visitors should be told what your site collects and why.

Our recommendation

Publish a clear Privacy Policy page and link it from every page footer. Describe the advertising technology you use. This is our general advice, not legal advice.

Watch out

Depending on where your visitors live, laws and Google's own requirements may mean you need a consent message. Look at the privacy and messaging options in your AdSense account, and ask a qualified professional about legal questions.

Words you will meet

Publisher ID
Your AdSense account identifier: pub- and 16 digits. In HTML it is written with ca- in front.
Ad unit
A block of code (an ins element plus a push call) that marks where an ad may appear.
Ad slot
The number that identifies one ad unit you created in AdSense.
Auto ads
A setting where Google picks ad positions for you. It needs only the head script.
ads.txt
A public text file at the root of your domain listing who may sell ads on your site.
Head
The <head> section of a page, where scripts and metadata live.
No fill
A normal moment when an ad request is made but no ad is returned for that slot.
